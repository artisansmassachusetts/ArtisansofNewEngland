A background in storytelling

My name is Daniel.

Artisans of New England grew from a lifelong passion for storytelling, documentation, education, and creative work. With a Bachelor of Science in Film and an Associate of Science in Broadcasting, I have spent much of my life studying how stories are captured, preserved, and shared.

Living in New Hampshire, Vermont, and Massachusetts gave me a close view of New England’s communities and traditions. Across those places, I became increasingly interested in the people behind creative work—not simply the finished product.

The most fascinating stories were often about how skills were learned, who taught them, and how knowledge was passed from one generation to another.

This community is for the makers,and the people who believe in making.

A philosophy of learning

Give people the grace to learn.

I have always believed in giving people the grace to learn and ask questions. Some of the most valuable lessons come from curiosity, patience, and the willingness to admit that we don’t know everything.

That belief helped inspire Artisans of New England. Every skill, trade, and craft depends on knowledge being shared from one generation to the next. When people are encouraged to learn, explore, and grow, those traditions continue to thrive.

Why Artisans of New England

Traditional skills deserve a living archive.

Over time, I realized that many traditional skills are at risk of being lost. Experienced craftspeople retire, businesses close, and valuable knowledge often disappears before it can be documented.

Artisans of New England was created to help preserve that knowledge through interviews, documentation, mentorship opportunities, apprenticeship initiatives, and educational resources.

A mission beyond objects

What we make is only part of the story.

While handcrafted work is important, the deeper mission is preserving the knowledge behind the work. Every artisan carries years of experience, problem-solving ability, practical wisdom, and lessons learned through practice.

“Craftsmanship is not only what we make. It is what we teach, what we share, and what we leave behind.”

Looking forward

A community with its sleeves rolled up.

My vision is to strengthen connections between mentors and learners, document traditional skills, celebrate local makers, and ensure that valuable knowledge remains accessible to future generations.

01 / DOCUMENTATION

Record the knowledge.

Interviews and thoughtful storytelling preserve the experience behind every craft.

02 / COMMUNITY

Make connections.

Mentorship and apprenticeship create pathways for knowledge to move between generations.

03 / POSSIBILITY

Open the door wider.

By combining storytelling, preservation, education, and community, we can honor both makers and those who wish to learn.
